# Texas-first market assessment and product strategy

Decision memo: 20 October 2026. This is a sourced desk survey, not customer
interviews, a willingness-to-pay study, or a completed competitive benchmark.
Vantage is independent of CropSight and belongs to [Lillie US](https://github.com/Ibekwemmanuel7/Lillie_US).

## Recommendation

Build Vantage as a Southern agriculture scouting and evidence product, launching
with independent advisers serving irrigated upland cotton in Hale and Floyd
counties, Texas. Sell a reviewed next-round inspection brief and the record of
what the scout found. A vegetation map alone is already a competitive commodity.
The differentiator to test is locally useful prioritization with transparent
observation quality, management context and measured field-confirmed usefulness.
This is a commercial hypothesis, not a demonstrated market gap.

## Market evidence

| Geography/year | Verified observation | Implication and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Texas, 2022 Census | 230,662 farms; 125,471,325 farm acres; $32.17bn products sold [1] | Large agricultural economy; includes ranching and operations outside the target market |
| Texas, 2024 survey | 5.95m upland-cotton planted acres; 2.90m harvested; $1.182bn production value [2] | Cotton is material and exposed to seasonal variability; planted-minus-harvested acres do not identify a particular cause |
| Hale, 2022 Census | 779 farms; 125,825 irrigated acres across all crops; $53.375m cotton/cottonseed sales [3] | Concentrated commercial crop context; not a count of irrigated cotton customers |
| Floyd, 2022 Census | 457 farms; 61,317 irrigated acres across all crops; $42.228m cotton/cottonseed sales [4] | Adjacent bounded launch territory; same intersection limitation |

The county irrigation totals sum to 187,142 acres, but include other crops and
systems. They are not the addressable cotton-pivot acreage. Farm sales are not
software spending. Do not multiply Texas farm counts by an assumed subscription
to claim a measured TAM. The verified annual crop table here is 2024; the attempted
2025 overview was inaccessible during this review. This is not a 2026 production forecast.

Build serviceable-market counts bottom-up: consultant portfolios → consented
fields → confirmed cotton seasons and pivot systems → eligible scouting weeks →
fresh-data availability → ability and willingness to pay. One field can rotate
crops, and multiple consultants can share relationships; deduplicate customers.
Consultant counts, reachable acres, churn, conversion and willingness to pay are
currently unknown.

## Competitive survey

These are vendor-described capabilities, not independently tested efficacy.
No verified like-for-like Texas quote or competitive market share was obtained.

| Product/alternative | Publicly described capabilities | What Vantage must test |
|---|---|---|
| FieldView | Field-health imagery and scouting workflow [5] | Can Vantage improve a consultant's next-round ordering beyond an existing imagery view? |
| EOSDA Crop Monitoring | Satellite-based problem-area scouting, assigned tasks, field reports and offline scouting [6] | Task assignment and offline maps are table stakes, not unique differentiators |
| CropX | Soil, weather, satellite and operations data; agronomy and irrigation recommendations [7] | Integrate context where permitted; do not compete through unsupported imagery-only irrigation advice |
| Ceres | Aerial water-stress/NDVI products, sensor integration and field workflows [8] | Establish whether public imagery is sufficient before buying higher-resolution observations; confirm Texas coverage and quotes |
| Existing adviser process | Visits, grower calls, photos, management knowledge and existing software | The actual baseline must be documented with each partner; do not assume it is inefficient |

EOSDA advertises multiple subscription tiers and enterprise customization [9].
This supports a packaged-software market, but does not establish a viable price
for Vantage. Vendor ROI claims cannot become Vantage evidence.

## Product shape

The adviser opens an ordered list for the next scouting round, sees observation
dates, evidence status and which zone changed, reviews comparable before/after
views and a trajectory, then records the visit and finding. Every recommendation
shows source, age, usable area, baseline and limitations. Management notes and crop
stage can explain benign differences. Missing/cloudy evidence leaves routine
scouting in place. No imagery alone establishes pests, disease or irrigation faults.

Launch package: boundary/season onboarding; weekly reviewed brief; refresh on
material new usable evidence; mobile/offline field checks; shareable evidence
report. Defer yield predictions, irrigation prescriptions, insurance adjudication,
autonomous management and generic AI farm chat. An LLM can later summarize
structured evidence with references; it must not invent diagnoses or measurements.

The first commercial relationship should be a seasonal paid pilot with the
consultant as proposed buyer and growers as beneficiaries/data owners. Discovery
must establish whether the consultant buys, resells or introduces direct buyers.
Offer a small base service fee plus an active-field or acreage band with a support
cap. Track imagery, compute, onboarding, agronomy review and customer support cost.

Illustrative price tests only: present equivalent seasonal bundles at $1,500,
$3,000 and $6,000 to qualified firms after understanding portfolio size. These
are interview hypotheses, not market rates, quotes or revenue forecasts. Test
whether acreage pricing or a firm subscription fits purchasing behavior. Never
infer payment intent from a free demo or an affirmative survey answer.

Value estimate = verified hours avoided × agreed labor cost + other documented
workflow value. Do not price against hypothetical yield saved. Report contribution
margin after human review and data costs; a low-cost public satellite feed does
not make service delivery free. Example only: five firms at $3,000/season yield
$15,000 gross seasonal revenue before costs; not an estimated obtainable market.

## Discovery and route to market

Proposed first interviews: 8–12 independent advisers, 5–8 growers and 2–3 potential
distribution partners across the launch area. None has been contacted by this work.
Ask to reconstruct the last scouting round, show current tools, identify missed
or wasted visits, specify delivery cutoff and cloud tolerance, establish boundary
and observation rights, identify budget authority, and react to a paid scope.
Record negative interviews and reasons for declining alongside positive ones.

Recruit through adviser introductions, local agricultural networks, cotton gins
and producer organizations; these are proposed channels, not partnerships or
endorsements. Show one reproducible local historical case, invite critical review,
and seek consent for a paid prospective comparison. Do not send outreach until
the owner authorizes it.

Pilot scope remains one firm, 3–5 growers and 20–40 fields. Measure findings per
visit, missed findings in sampled unflagged zones, adviser time, availability,
changed inspection order, costs and renewal at an actual price. Freeze success
criteria with the adviser after development data and before evaluation. Pause or
change cadence if fresh imagery cannot support the agreed workflow.

## Southern expansion

Texas is multiple agronomic markets. Expand within High Plains cotton first,
then evaluate other Texas row crops only with partner data and crop-specific
season rules. Coastal cotton/rice needs cloud/flood and harvest-specific methods;
Lower Rio Grande Valley crops need a separate water/management context. Later
Louisiana, Arkansas and Mississippi row crops require separate local validation.
Pasture and specialty crops are separate opportunities, not automatic extensions.
Forestry remains with Vepris; Vaughan owns event flood layers and Velo ongoing
water context. Those integrations are planned, not connected services.
